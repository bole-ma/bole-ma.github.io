I earned FAA Part 107 certification, collected ≈1,000 UAV images, completed calibration, and tested pretrained models and a compact U-Net.

The experiment changed the plan. Pretrained predictions differed from reference measurements; our own data could not cover the required distances and viewpoints within the available budget.

How this informs my current depth research →

Purdue received $250,000 across white-paper and semifinal awards. Per-target errors were not disclosed; the award does not establish measurement accuracy. Target size, orientation, and camera and pixel measurements remain sources of error.

Technical background and method comparison

The official challenge evaluates ranges of 2.5–10 km. I used camera-axis depth as the working quantity; conversion to line-of-sight range requires intrinsics and target image position. These are task specifications, not performance achieved by our submission.

Relative depth alone does not supply absolute distance. My method screening concerned feasibility with our inputs, rather than ruling out a method in all settings.

For the final baseline, averaging can reduce some measurement variability but cannot remove shared target-size bias. No deblurring or lens-distortion correction was applied.
